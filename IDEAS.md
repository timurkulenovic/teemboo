# Teemboo — Idea Playbook

> Minimalist t-shirts tied to pop-culture, sold DTC.
> A consolidated record of the concept, business plan, motif library, and
> generated designs from the founding brainstorm.

---

## 1. The concept

**Teemboo** is a direct-to-consumer label making premium organic-cotton
t-shirts with quiet, design-led graphics inspired by film, music, and
internet culture.

**The thesis:** pop-culture merch is a crowded category, but it splits into
two camps —

- **Cheap print-on-demand marketplaces** (TeePublic, Redbubble) — maximalist
  artist designs on cheap blanks.
- **Licensed illustrative brands** (Super Yaki, Bravado) — bold, fandom-forward
  graphics at mid quality.

Neither owns the **premium + minimal** intersection. Teemboo targets that gap
with original, design-led motifs on heavyweight organic cotton, sold DTC at
full margin.

### Positioning in one line
Pop-culture resonance delivered through restraint. One motif, one ink color,
premium substrate. The shirt reads as a design object first and a reference
second — wearable by people who never saw the film.

## 2. Business plan (summary)

### Headline targets — Year 1
| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$467k |
| Gross margin | 67% |
| Average order value (AOV) | $42 |
| Customer acquisition cost (CAC), Year 1 | $20 |
| LTV : CAC (steady state) | 6:1 |
| Year 1 net | ~−$37k (deliberate investment year) |
| Break-even | ~Month 18, as CAC falls to ~$12 |

### Product
Heavyweight 240–300gsm organic cotton tees, garment-dyed, made in Portugal.
Graphics are single-color line art — a feather, a silhouette, a glyph — never
a busy illustration or a logo dump.

### Audience
Design-literate 22–38 year olds who reference film, music, and memes but
reject loud branding. They already buy Cuts, Cos, and Aesop — they want a tee
that signals taste, not fandom volume.

### Revenue mix (share of Year 1 revenue)
- **Essentials** (core blanks) — 55% — funds the brand.
- **Collections** (pop-culture capsules) — 30% — the cultural hook.
- **Limited** (licensed / numbered, ≤200) — 15% — scarcity and margin.

### Design principles
1. One motif per shirt, one ink color, no halftone.
2. Reference is felt, not shouted — no film titles, no logos.
3. Every design must read as a graphic first, reference second.
4. Releases in monthly capsules of 3–4 designs, not weekly drops.
5. Limited tier is numbered (≤200) and retires after sell-through.

### Unit economics (per order, Year 1)
| Line item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Average order value (AOV) | $42.00 |
| Blank organic garment | −$8.00 |
| DTG print | −$4.00 |
| Packaging & label | −$2.00 |
| **Cost of goods (COGS)** | **−$14.00** |
| **Gross profit** | **$28.00 (67%)** |
| Shipping (blended, free > $75) | −$6.00 |
| Payment processing (2.9% + $0.30) | −$1.50 |
| Acquisition cost (CAC), Year 1 | −$20.00 |
| Contribution before fixed costs | $0.50 |
| Fixed cost allocation per order | −$3.10 |
| **Net per order, Year 1** | **−$2.60** |

> Year 1 is a deliberate investment year. Contribution per order is roughly
> break-even once CAC is paid; fixed costs push each order ~$2.60 negative.
> The plan buys repeat customers and owned-audience leverage so that, as
> blended CAC falls toward $12 and repeat rate clears 35%, orders turn
> positive around Month 18.

### Customer acquisition
**Channel mix (new customers, Year 1):**
- Paid social (Meta, TikTok) — 40%
- Organic / content — 25%
- Referral / word of mouth — 15%
- Email / owned — 12%
- Search / SEO — 8%

**Playbook:**
- Creator seeding with cinephile / music-critic micro-influencers.
- "Design of the month" editorial content over paid clips.
- Email welcome flow + capsule drop alerts to owned list.
- Referral: $10 store credit each way, capped at 25% of orders.
- SEO long-tail on "minimalist [film] t-shirt" once catalog deepens.

### Roadmap
| Quarter | Phase | Key moves | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Launch | Store live, 9 SKUs, paid social test, first 600 orders | Proof of demand |
| Q2 | Optimize | Repeat-rate tracking, email flows, 2 capsule drops | CAC < $22, repeat > 25% |
| Q3 | Scale | First licensed capsule signed, intl. shipping, wholesale pilot | 1,000 orders/mo |
| Q4 | Expand | Second designer, 2nd supplier qualified, Series seed close | Break-even in sight |

### Funding ask
**~$60k seed** — covers ~$37k Year-1 cumulative burn, ~$15k initial inventory &
print setup, and an ~$8k buffer. Target close: end of Q1.

## 3. Motif library

The rule for every motif: a **generic object** that the in-group reads as a
reference and everyone else reads as a clean graphic. **Never** the title,
character name, logo, likeness, or quoted line — unless the source is in the
public domain.

### Film & TV
- **Drifting feather** — *Forrest Gump*. ✅ generated (see §4).
- **Paper boat in the rain** — *It* (the Georgie scene) and childhood-loss
  broadly. Generic object, very evocative.
- **Red balloon** — *It* / "you'll float too." Generic; keep it a plain
  balloon, no text.
- **Yellow hazmat suit** — *Breaking Bad*. Generic garment silhouette.
- **Single white chess king** — *The Queen's Gambit*. A chess piece is
  unprotectable.
- **Queen of hearts playing card** — *Alice in Wonderland* (public domain) and
  card-game culture generally.
- **Plain red rose** — *Beauty and the Beast* (the enchanted rose). Keep it a
  rose, not the glass-dome version, which is the film's specific design.
- **Glass slipper / poisoned apple / spinning wheel** — Cinderella, Snow White,
  Sleeping Beauty. All public-domain fairy tales, all generic objects.
- **Red stapler** — *Office Space*. An ordinary office object; the in-group
  gets it instantly.
- **Wayfarer sunglasses silhouette** — *Risky Business* / *Blues Brothers* era.
  Generic eyewear.
- **Plain briefcase** — *Pulp Fiction* (the glowing case). Keep it a closed
  briefcase, no glow.
- **Cherry pie + black coffee** — *Twin Peaks*. Everyday objects; together
  they signal the show to fans.

### Music
- **Single banana** — Velvet Underground & Nico cover. A yellow banana is a
  fruit, not protectable; one of the most recognizable music references for the
  design-literate. ✅ proposed hero.
- **Vinyl 45 adapter (the small spacer)** — crate-digging culture; instantly
  recognizable to record people, opaque to outsiders.
- **Guitar pick** — generic, music-lifestyle.
- **Walkman / cassette silhouette** — 80s era broadly, not one title.
- **Neon arrow sign** — synthwave / 80s mood, no single rights holder.
- **Single drumstick** — generic.
- **Lightning bolt** — evokes Bowie / *Aladdin Sane*. A plain bolt is a generic
  shape; the specific face makeup is what's protected, and we're not using it.
- *Skip:* Nirvana smiley, Dark Side of the Moon prism — both are specific
  protected designs.

### Literature (mostly public domain = safest tier)
- **Raven silhouette** — *The Raven*, Poe, fully PD; you can even print the
  title or a line. ✅ generated (see §4).
- **White whale** — *Moby-Dick*, PD; title usable.
- **Scarlet letter "A"** — *The Scarlet Letter*, PD; title usable.
- **Green light** — *Gatsby*; the novel is PD, the 2013 film isn't, so keep it
  abstract. ✅ proposed hero.
- **Red hunting cap** — *The Catcher in the Rye*. Book is in copyright (no
  title), but a plain cap silhouette is safe.
- **Conch shell** — *Lord of the Flies*. In copyright, so motif only, no title.
- **Round glasses** — evokes *Harry Potter*. Glasses alone aren't a likeness,
  but the round-style is close to the character — keep it generic, not the
  exact HP frame.
- **Golden ticket** — *Charlie and the Chocolate Factory*. Book's PD status
  varies by country; a plain ticket motif is safe everywhere, the phrase
  "golden ticket" is not.
- **Single key** — *The Secret Garden*, PD; generic object.

### Internet & computing
- **Progress bar stuck at 99%** — the universal "almost done" joke; fully
  generic.
- **Cursor arrow** — generic, web-native.
- **Single "@" symbol** — generic.
- **Dial-up modem** — era nostalgia, generic.
- **Custom-drawn ghost** — evokes emoji culture; *caution:* actual emoji
  glyphs are copyrighted by the font maker, so draw your own.
- **"404"** — generic phrase.
- **Floppy disk** (the "save" icon) — evokes early-web nostalgia for a 25–35
  audience; completely generic.

### Gaming & tabletop
- **Single D20** — D&D; the 20-sided die shape is generic geometry (Wizards of
  the Coast's trademarks are on names/marks, not the polyhedron).
- **Plain six-sided die** — generic, tabletop.
- **Arcade joystick** — 80s arcade era, generic.
- **Pixel heart** — generic across gaming.
- **Plain mushroom** — *caution:* the red-and-white spotted Mario mushroom is
  trademarked; use a different colorway or a plain fungus.
- **"Game Over"** — generic phrase, not protectable.

### Science & space
- **Saturn with rings** — generic planet.
- **Rocket silhouette** — generic, mid-century space optimism.
- **Bohr-model atom** — generic.
- **Accretion disc (abstract)** — evokes *Interstellar*'s black hole; keep it
  abstract, not the film's specific render.
- **DNA helix** — generic.
- **Generic constellation (dots + lines)** — generic.

### Objects of an era (lowest IP risk — no single rights holder)
- **Rotary phone** — mid-century.
- **Typewriter** — writer culture.
- **Film SLR camera** — generic.
- **VHS tape** — 80s/90s.
- **Boombox** — 80s.
- **Lava lamp** — 70s.
- **Hi-fi rotary knob** — audiophile.
- **Martini glass with olive** — Mad Men era, generic.

### Food & drink
- **Martini glass** (above).
- **Plain donut** — generic; *caution:* pink-frosted-with-sprinkles edges
  toward *The Simpsons*.
- **Single slice of pizza** — NYC culture, generic.
- **Popsicle** — summer nostalgia, generic.

### Sport
- **Red card** — generic.
- **Basketball silhouette** — generic.
- **Marathon bib (number pinned)** — generic.

### Proposed capsules
- **Hero capsule (launch):** feather (film, wistful), banana (music, playful),
  green light (literature, aspirational) — tests the thesis across three
  domains and moods.
- **Second capsule:** paper boat, vinyl 45 adapter, raven, D20, rotary phone —
  tests film, music, literature, gaming, and era-nostalgia in one drop.

## 5. IP & legal notes (important)

This is **strategy, not legal advice.** "Evocative but generic" can still draw
a takedown if a rights holder argues the motif functions as a trademark or
implies endorsement. Concrete guidance:

- **Avoid** protected names, character names, logos, likenesses, and quoted
  dialogue unless you have a license.
- **Lead** with original "inspired-by" motifs that avoid those protected
  elements.
- **Lean hardest** on the public-domain literature tier (raven, whale,
  scarlet A) where titles and quotes are usable outright.
- **Licensed tier:** budget $8–15k per title and 8–12 weeks lead time. Convert
  proven demand from the original motifs into properly licensed capsules.
- Have a lawyer sanity-check the shortlist before printing — IP is fact-
  specific.
